# Barnhaus Design Rules — Complete Reference
*Based on actual Barnhaus floor plans analyzed 2026-03-04. Read before every build.*

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT BARNHAUS ACTUALLY BUILDS

Barnhaus Steel Builders builds steel-frame custom homes (barndominiums) with these consistent characteristics:

- **Aesthetic:** Modern, clean, heavy use of glass on rear/view wall, dark metal exterior, large overhangs
- **Ceiling heights:** Vaulted great room is the hero space. 10–11ft everywhere else.
- **Roofs:** Gable roofs are most common (stone, standing-seam metal). Flat/shed on garage or secondary volumes.
- **Master suite:** Always elaborate — 4–5 components (bed + bath + his + hers + optional sitting)
- **Open plan living core:** Kitchen/dining/great room are always one open volume
- **Signature feature:** Covered porch or outdoor courtyard integrated into the L or U shape

---

## SECTION 2: ZONE LAYOUT — THE BARNHAUS PATTERN

Every Barnhaus plan follows this zone sequence:

```
[MASTER SUITE] ──── [TRANSITION] ──── [GREAT ROOM / KITCHEN] ──── [BED WING] ──── [SERVICE/GARAGE]
   (dead end,           (hall,            (open plan,                (clustered,       (mudroom,
    private,            breeze,            vaulted ceiling,           jack/jill         pantry,
    rear corner)        storage)           hero space)                baths)            garage)
```

**Critical zone rules:**
- Master is ALWAYS at the END farthest from secondary bedrooms
- Master ALWAYS has direct patio/outdoor access (sliding door or French door)
- Public zone (great room) is in the CENTER or closest to front entry
- Garage connects via mudroom → pantry → kitchen (service path)
- Office sits adjacent to the great room (semi-private — has a door but feels connected to living)

---

## SECTION 3: MASTER SUITE — THE FIVE-COMPONENT STANDARD

High-end Barnhaus plans (confirmed in analyzed plans) include:
1. **Master Bedroom** (200–320 SF) — largest bedroom, rear-facing, patio slider
2. **Master Bath** (140–200 SF) — walk-in shower (min 4×6), dual vanity (min 6ft), freestanding tub OR rain shower niche
3. **His Closet** (60–100 SF) — walk-in, accessed from bedroom or bath
4. **Hers Closet** (80–140 SF) — larger walk-in, accessed from bedroom or bath
5. **Sitting Room** (100–160 SF) — optional, reading nook / morning coffee space within the master zone

**Layout pattern (confirmed from analyzed plans):**
```
[His Closet] ──── [Master Bed] ──── [Sitting/Flex]
                       │
                  [Master Bath]
                       │
               [Hers Closet]
```
- Closets flank the master bedroom or master bath
- Master bath is directly off the bedroom — never through another room
- His/hers closets: accessed from bath OR bedroom, NOT from a hallway
- Toilet is in a separate water closet or screened from the main vanity area
- Linen closet adjacent to bath is common

---

## SECTION 4: GREAT ROOM / KITCHEN / DINING — THE OPEN CORE

These three spaces are ONE open volume. Do not put walls between them.

**Arrangement (confirmed pattern):**
```
[Kitchen — back wall/corner] ──── [Dining] ──── [Great Room]
       │                                               │
   [Pantry/Butler]                           [Rear Patio Slider]
       │
  [Mudroom/Garage path]
```

**Kitchen position:** Always in the rear corner (against back wall). Kitchen back is against the deepest wall. This gives the cook a view across the dining and living toward the front — they see everything.

**Island:** Present in every Barnhaus kitchen 200 SF or larger. Island = prep + seating (4–6 bar stools). Position island perpendicular to kitchen back wall, 4ft clearance on all sides.

**Sightlines from kitchen:**
- Must see: front entry (who's arriving)
- Must see: great room / living area (kids, guests)
- Must see: rear patio / yard (outdoor entertaining)

**Pantry/Butler Pantry:** Located immediately adjacent to kitchen, on the path between garage and kitchen. Never more than 10ft from the kitchen.

**Vaulted ceiling over great room:** The gable roof creates a vaulted space. This is the hero architectural moment. In Revit, the room area is still calculated at floor level — the vaulted ceiling is a visual feature of the roof model.

---

## SECTION 5: SECONDARY BEDROOM WING

**Clustering rule:** ALL secondary bedrooms are in one zone. Never separated.

**Bathroom allocation:**
- 2 secondary beds → 1 full bath (shared or jack-and-jill) + 1 half bath
- 3 secondary beds → 2 full baths (one shared, one en-suite to the largest) 
- 4 secondary beds → 2 full baths on L2 (one per pair)

**Jack-and-jill pattern (confirmed in analyzed plans):**
```
[Bed 2] ──door──> [Bath] <──door── [Bed 3]
```
Both rooms have a door INTO the shared bath. No hallway access to bath — it's shared between the two bedrooms.

**Every secondary bedroom must have:**
- Door to hallway or landing (NEVER door to another bedroom unless jack-and-jill bath)
- Walk-in closet (5×5 minimum) OR reach-in (6ft wide minimum)
- At least one window (48"×48" minimum)

---

## SECTION 6: GARAGE AND SERVICE ZONE

**Garage standard sizes:**
- 1-car: 12×22 to 14×24 (264–336 SF)
- 2-car: 22×22 to 24×26 (484–624 SF) ← most common Barnhaus
- 3-car: 30×22 to 34×26 (660–884 SF)

**Wall height:** 12ft minimum (to allow 10ft overhead door). Use 14ft for RV.

**Mudroom (required when garage is attached):**
- Size: 8×10 to 10×14 (80–140 SF)
- Contains: bench + hooks, often W/D hookups, utility sink
- Connects: Garage → Mudroom → Kitchen/Pantry
- This is the EVERYDAY ENTRY PATH for the family

**Butler pantry (Barnhaus standard):**
- Size: 8×10 to 10×14 (80–140 SF)
- Positioned between mudroom/garage and kitchen
- Contains: second sink, wine fridge, extra counter space, pantry storage
- Opens with a 30" door (discreet entry)

---

## SECTION 7: SPECIAL ROOMS FROM BRIEF

**Bonus Room:** Flex space. On L2 or at end of secondary wing. Min 180 SF. Good for game room, home theater, play room. Often has its own closet to allow conversion to bedroom later.

**Office/Study:** Semi-private. Adjacent to great room but has a door. Own closet makes it an official room. 140–220 SF. Natural light preferred (east-facing for morning light).

**Media Room:** Isolated from main circulation (for sound). Interior room or with acoustic wall treatment. Min 180 SF. L-shape or tiered seating layout.

**Safe Room / Wine Room / Salon:** Specialty rooms — place per client preference, usually accessed from a hallway, not open to living.

**Sitting Room (Master):** Part of master suite zone. Morning coffee space. 100–160 SF. Comfortable chairs, often has TV niche. May have private access to patio.

**Breezeway:** Covered walkway connecting two building volumes (often garage to house, or detached structure). 8–10ft wide, can be screened or open.

## SECTION 8: PORCH, PATIO, AND OUTDOOR ROOMS

**Covered rear porch (most common):**
- Size: 12–16ft deep, full width of rear wall
- U panel metal soffits (Barnhaus standard finish)
- Fans, lights, outdoor speakers
- Connected to great room via slider or pocket door

**Master patio:**
- Private patio off master bedroom
- 8–12ft deep, full width of master rear wall
- Accessed by slider directly from master bedroom
- Can be hot tub pad

**Front porch/entry:**
- 6–8ft deep, centered on front door
- Covered, provides shelter at entry
- Architectural detail — sets the tone of the home

**Courtyard (L or U shape):**
- The sheltered inner corner of an L or U footprint
- This is the Barnhaus signature outdoor space
- Both interior-facing walls should have sliders/glass to borrow the courtyard
- Often covered patio on one or both sides

---

## SECTION 9: TWO-STORY SPECIFIC RULES

**Level 1 contains:** Master suite, great room, kitchen, dining, service zone
**Level 2 contains:** Secondary bedrooms, bonus room, office, bathrooms, landing

**Staircase placement:**
- Between great room and secondary bed wing
- Never at the master suite end
- Width: 4ft (4.5ft preferred)
- Goes up ~12–13ft over a 13–15ft horizontal run at standard rise

**L2 landing (critical):**
- Hub of all L2 circulation — every L2 room has its door here
- Minimum 8×10 (80 SF), 10×12 preferred
- Should have open railing overlooking great room below (visual connection)
- Natural light via window at end of landing or skylight

**L1 room heights for Revit rooms:**
- L1 walls: H=11ft (z=0 to z=11)
- L2 floor: z=11
- For L1 room labels, use `upper_limit_level="Level 2.0"` (z=11 = actual top of L1 walls)

**L2 room heights for Revit rooms:**
- L2 walls: H=10ft (z=11 to z=21)
- L2 Roof: z=21
- For L2 room labels, use `upper_limit_level="L2 Roof"` (z=21)

## SECTION 10: ROOF RULES

**Gable roof (most common):**
- Ridge runs along the LONG axis of the volume
- Pitch: 4:12 (0.333) for standard, 6:12 (0.5) for steeper Barnhaus look
- The slope creates the vaulted great room ceiling
- Roof covers full footprint including overhang (2–3ft minimum)

**Shed roof:**
- Single slope. Often used on garages or secondary volumes (porches)
- Also used for modern "flat-look" on wings
- Pitch: 1:12 (0.083) to 3:12 (0.25)

**Flat roof:**
- Garage and covered porches
- Pitch: 0.01 (minimal for drainage)

**L-shape with two gable roofs:**
- Each wing gets its own gable roof
- Ridges are perpendicular (one E-W, one N-S)
- Roofs meet at a valley at the inner corner
- In Revit: create two separate make_roof() calls, attach walls of each wing to its respective roof

## SECTION 11: CEILING HEIGHT IMPLEMENTATION IN REVIT

| Level | Room | Wall Height | upper_limit_level |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 (z=0) | All L1 rooms | H=11 (z=0→11) | `"Level 2.0"` |
| L2 (z=11) | All L2 rooms | H=10 (z=11→21) | `"L2 Roof"` |
| Garage | Garage | H=12 (z=0→12) | `"Garage Roof"` |

**Important:** `"L1 Roof"` level at z=10 is WRONG for L1 room bounding when walls are H=11. The room needs to be bounded UP TO THE TOP of the walls (z=11 = Level 2.0). Use `"Level 2.0"` as upper limit for all L1 rooms.

## SECTION 12: WINDOW PLACEMENT RULES

**Grouping:** Barnhaus windows come in bands, not isolated holes. Group 2–3 windows together on view walls.

**Rear/view wall (south or courtyard-facing):**
- Maximum glass
- 72"×36" fixed windows, grouped in pairs or triples
- Large slider centered on great room wall (hero moment)
- Master bedroom: 2× 72"×36" flanking slider or French door

**Front/street wall (north-facing):**
- Restrained — don't expose the home's interior to the street
- Small symmetrical windows flanking front door: 48"×48" max
- No picture windows on front face

**Side walls:**
- Kitchen east: 60"×30" above counter
- Office: 48"×48"
- Bathrooms: Awning type, 24"×72" or `Window-Awning-Single`, at high sill (5ft+)
- Clerestory (high strip): 72"×24" fixed

**Window sill heights:**
- L1 standard: z=2.5 (2.5ft above floor)
- L2 standard: z=13.5 (2.5ft above L2 floor which is z=11)
- Bath privacy: z=5.0 on L1, z=15.5 on L2
- Clerestory: z=7.0 on L1

## SECTION 14: THINGS TO NEVER DO

❌ Never place a bedroom door opening directly into another bedroom  
❌ Never make someone walk through a bedroom to reach another room  
❌ Never put the master suite adjacent to secondary bedrooms (no shared wall)  
❌ Never put a bathroom where you can see the toilet from the entry door  
❌ Never put a garage that connects directly to a living room without a mudroom  
❌ Never design a great room under 280 SF — it won't feel like a great room  
❌ Never put the staircase at the master suite end of a 2-story plan  
❌ Never skip the butler pantry when kitchen_features.butlerPantry = true  
❌ Never design a kitchen island with less than 4ft clearance on all sides  
❌ Never place L2 bedroom doors on exterior walls  
❌ Never use 84" door height — template only has 96" standard interior doors  
❌ Never guess at window/door type names — use REVIT_TEMPLATE.md exact strings

## Section 16 — H-Shape Exterior Wall Pattern (CONFIRMED 2026-03-07)

H-shape has three zones: left wing, center bridge, right wing.

### Exterior Wall Build Order
1. **Left wing**: `create_rect_exterior(x0, y0, x_bridge, y1, ..., skip_faces=['east'])` — skip east face (open to bridge)
2. **Left wing partial east**: two manual walls at x=bridge_x, for y=y0→bridge_y_north and y=bridge_y_south→y1 — draw top-to-bottom (y1→y0) for correct east-facing
3. **Center bridge**: `create_rect_exterior(x_bridge, bridge_y0, x_bridge2, bridge_y1, ..., skip_faces=['east','west'])` — skip both sides (open to wings). Height = center bridge height.
4. **Right wing**: `create_rect_exterior(x_bridge2, y0, x1, y1, ..., skip_faces=['west'])` — skip west face
5. **Right wing partial west**: two manual walls at x=bridge_x2, y=y0→bridge_y_north and y=bridge_y_south→y1 — draw bottom-to-top (y0→y1) for correct west-facing
6. Always call `verify_wall_facing` on each manual partial wall

### Clerestory Walls (when center bridge is taller than wings)
When center_bridge height > wing height, add clerestory walls on the bridge sides (x=left_bridge and x=right_bridge) spanning the full bridge depth (y=bridge_y0 to bridge_y1):
- Start z = wing_height (top of wing walls/roof)
- Height = center_bridge_height − wing_height
- Attach these walls to the center bridge roof

```python
# Example: wings=11ft, bridge=16ft → clerestory starts z=11, height=5
cb_west = create_wall(x_bridge, bridge_y0, 11, x_bridge, bridge_y1, 11, LEVEL, EXT, height=5)
verify_wall_facing(cb_west, -1, 0, 'CB-West-Clerestory')
cb_east = create_wall(x_bridge2, bridge_y1, 11, x_bridge2, bridge_y0, 11, LEVEL, EXT, height=5)
verify_wall_facing(cb_east, +1, 0, 'CB-East-Clerestory')
attach_walls_to_roof([cb_west, cb_east], center_bridge_roof_id)
```

### Roof Levels for Multi-Height H-Shape
- Wings: standard level (e.g. "Level 2.0" at z=11)
- Center bridge: create a new level at the bridge wall height (e.g. `revit.create_level` → "CB Roof" at z=16)
- Use `make_roof(... level_name="CB Roof")` so bridge roof base sits at correct elevation

## Section 17 — Fixture & Cabinet Placement Rules

### Two-Script Rule
ALWAYS split builds into two scripts:
1. `build_[id].py` — walls, floors, doors, windows, roofs, stairs, room labels (labels go AFTER interior walls in Stage 2 — walls must exist to bound rooms)
2. `fixtures_[id].py` — cabinets, appliances, plumbing, furniture  
Run structure script first (including room labels), verify it completes, THEN run fixtures.

### Rotation Test (MANDATORY before first fixture run in Revit)
Before running a full fixture script in a fresh Revit model, place ONE test cabinet:
```python
place_fixture("Base Cabinet-Double Door & 1 Drawer", "36\"",
              X, WALL_Y - 1.5, 0, LEVEL, rotation=0, label="TEST-r0")
```
Check in Revit: does the cabinet face INTO the room?
- Yes → rotation=0 is correct for north walls; document and proceed
- No → try rotation=180; update REVIT_TEMPLATE.md with confirmed value

### Cabinet Offset Formula
Back of cabinet must be flush to INTERIOR face of wall (not floating, not through wall):
- Base cabinets (2ft deep): place center at wall_coord ± 1.5
- Upper cabinets (1ft deep): place center at wall_coord ± 0.5
- Appliances (~2.5ft): place center at wall_coord ± 1.25
- Signs: north/east walls subtract; south/west walls add

### Cabinet Continuity Rules
- No gaps in cabinet runs — place every 3ft along the wall
- Sink base (`Base Cabinet-Double Door Sink Unit`) placed at same Y as other bases
- Appliance (range, DW) placed at appliance Y offset, sink unit behind it at base Y offset
- Leave 4ft min clearance between island and any cabinet leg
- Island has sink (`Sink Kitchen-Island`) only if ≥ 6ft long

### Bathroom Fixture Rules
- Every full bath: toilet + sink/vanity + tub OR shower
- Master bath: walk-in shower (brief determines tub presence)
- All fixtures placed against walls — toilet tank to wall, vanity back to wall
- Vanity origin placement: same offset formula as cabinets (base ± 1.5)

### Checklist — Fixtures
- [ ] Rotation tested and confirmed before full run
- [ ] All cabinet backs flush to wall interior face (no gaps, no penetrations)
- [ ] Cabinet faces pointing INTO the room
- [ ] Kitchen: range + hood + DW + sink on back wall
- [ ] Kitchen: fridge at end of one leg
- [ ] Island clearance ≥ 4ft all sides
- [ ] Master bath: shower + dual vanity + toilet; tub only if brief specifies
- [ ] Laundry room: stacked washer/dryer if brief says so

### Door Clearance — MANDATORY (confirmed 2026-03-06)
- **NEVER place fixtures/cabinets in front of a doorway** — they block the swing and the opening
- Rule: fixture center must be ≥ (door_width/2 + 2ft) away from door center position
- `place_against_wall()` has built-in check via `door_positions=[(wall_coord, pos_along, width_ft, clearance_ft)]`
- **Always pass door_positions when calling place_against_wall** for any wall that has a door
- Door swing direction matters:
  - If door swings AWAY from the wall face you're placing on → no conflict (e.g. kitchen cabs on kitchen side of x=50 wall, doors swing into laundry/master on east side)
  - If door swings INTO the same space you're placing on → conflict, must clear
- Corner rule: don't place fridge/tall units in the corner where two walls meet if a door is on either wall — leave 3ft of clearance from every corner with a door

### Checklist Addition — Door Clearance
- [ ] Every wall with a door: pass door_positions to place_against_wall
- [ ] Corner fixtures: verify no door within 3ft of corner on either wall
- [ ] Fridge: always at END of a run, never adjacent to a door or corner-adjacent to a door

## Section 18 — Porch Roof Slope Rule (CONFIRMED 2026-03-07)

Porch shed roofs ALWAYS slope AWAY from the house — high at the house wall, low at the outer post line.
Posts are always at the LOW (outer) end. No Top Offset needed on any porch posts.

- Back porch (house wall is at large-y side of porch): `shed_low_edge=0` → low at y=y0 (outer edge)
- Front porch (house wall is at small-y side of porch): `shed_low_edge=2` → low at y=y1 (outer edge)
- `place_porch_posts()` no longer needs a top_offset — posts are always at low end

## Section 19 — Floor Slab Rules (CONFIRMED 2026-03-07)

### The Golden Rule
**Every shared edge between two adjacent floor zones must have exp=False on BOTH sides.**
Only expand (exp=True) on edges that face true open ground (no adjacent floor zone).

### exp_* flag mapping
- `exp_s` → controls y0 (smaller y) edge expansion
- `exp_n` → controls y1 (larger y) edge expansion
- `exp_w` → controls x0 (smaller x) edge expansion
- `exp_e` → controls x1 (larger x) edge expansion

### H-Shape with Breezeways — Correct Flags

| Zone | x | y | exp_w | exp_s | exp_e | exp_n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Left wing | 0–22 | 8–44 | True | True | **False** | True |
| Left breezeway | 22–30 | 8–44 | **False** | True | **False** | True |
| Center bridge | 30–58 | 14–38 | **False** | **False** | **False** | **False** |
| Right breezeway | 58–66 | 8–44 | **False** | True | **False** | True |
| Right wing | 66–88 | 8–44 | **False** | True | True | True |
| Back porch | 30–58 | 0–14 | **False** | True | **False** | **False** |
| Front porch | 30–58 | 38–48 | **False** | **False** | **False** | True |

### Why Center Bridge is All False
The bridge's north (y=14) and south (y=38) edges touch the porches, and east (x=58) and west (x=30) edges touch the breezeways — all 4 sides are shared.

### Why Porches are False on x edges
Even though there are no walls at x=30/x=58 for the porches, the breezeway floors are adjacent at those x coords (overlapping y range). Always set exp_w=False, exp_e=False on porches in H-shape.

### For Other Shapes
- **Rectangle**: all 4 edges expand (exp_w=True, exp_s=True, exp_e=True, exp_n=True)
- **L-shape**: corner zone — 2 shared edges False, 2 outer edges True
- **U-shape**: courtyard zone — typically all False; wings have 1 False each
- **Attached porch**: shared wall edge always False; outer edge True

## Section 20 — 2-Story L-Shape Rules (CONFIRMED 2026-03-07)

### L2 Floor Placement
- L2 sits ONLY above the service zone portion of the L — NEVER above a single-story wing
- The service zone floor at L1 already covers the garage area — DO NOT add a separate garage slab
- **One slab rule**: if two zones share the same footprint at the same level, use ONE floor slab. Never double up.

### Garage Under L2
- When L2 sits above the garage, the L2 floor slab IS the garage ceiling — no separate garage roof
- A separate garage roof + L2 floor creates a phantom void room between them in Revit
- Garage walls (GAR-N, GAR-E) should be constrained to Level 2.0 and attached to the L2 floor

### L1 Walls Under L2 Floor — MANDATORY ATTACHMENT
- L1 walls constrained to Level 2.0 (z=11) poke through the L2 floor slab underside
- After creating the L2 floor, ALWAYS call:
  ```python
  attach_walls_to_roof([all_l1_walls_under_l2], l2_floor_id)
  ```
- This includes: service zone exterior walls, garage walls, and the main wing/service zone divider wall

### Wall Facing — Service Zone
- Service zone exterior walls often face wrong direction after create_wall()
- ALWAYS call `flip_wall()` after creating SVC-W, SVC-N, SVC-S if they face inward
- Better: use `verify_wall_facing()` on ALL exterior walls after creation

### No Separate Garage Roof When L2 is Above
- If L2 floor covers the garage footprint → skip make_roof() for garage
- Only add garage roof if garage is fully single-story with NO floor above it

## Section 21 — Porch Rules (CONFIRMED 2026-03-07)

### Porches Are Always Open
- Front and rear porches NEVER have side walls — only a roof and posts
- Only allowed wall: a low fascia wall on the far outer edge (optional)
- Side walls box in the porch and kill the design — delete FP-W and FP-E always

### Porch Posts — MANDATORY in build script
- `place_porch_posts()` MUST be called in the build script, not as an afterthought
- Comment "posts only" in a design note means `place_porch_posts()` goes in the code
- Front porch: 2 posts at outer corners
- Rear porch: posts every ~14ft along outer edge

### Porch Post Placement Formula (CONFIRMED)
```python
place_porch_posts(
    post_xs=[...],
    post_y=outer_edge_y,      # exact outer floor edge
    level=L1,
    porch_depth=N,            # ft from house wall to outer edge
    wall_height=11,           # house wall plate height
    shed_slopes_toward_larger_y=True/False  # True if outer edge is at larger y
)
```
- Posts auto-offset 0.25ft inward from floor edge (COL_HALF) so they don't overhang
- Top Offset = -(porch_depth × pitch) so tops meet roof underside exactly
- Do NOT subtract roof thickness — that makes posts too short

### Porch Roof Boundary — Pull Back from House Wall
- Porch roof boundary must NOT reach the house wall centerline
- Pull back 0.4ft from the house wall on the house-adjacent edge
- Back porch (house at y=0): use y=-0.4 as the south boundary (not y=0)
- Front porch (house at y=52): use y=52.4 as the north boundary (not y=52)

## Section 28 — Wall Coordinate Offset (CONFIRMED 2026-03-08)

### The Problem
`location_line="exterior"` in `revit.create_wall` sets the parameter label but does NOT shift the wall position after creation. The wall is always placed with its CENTERLINE at the given coordinate.

### The Fix — Inset Wall Coordinates
When placing exterior walls so the exterior face aligns with the slab edge:
- Slab boundary polygon = true exterior outline (e.g. x=0, y=8)
- Wall centerline = slab edge + half wall thickness INWARD
- Half thickness of `Wall 7.5" EXT PBR` = 0.3125ft (3.75")

**Offsets per wall face direction:**
- South wall (exterior faces south, y=const): wall y_coord = slab_y + 0.3125
- North wall (exterior faces north, y=const): wall y_coord = slab_y - 0.3125
- West wall (exterior faces west, x=const):  wall x_coord = slab_x + 0.3125
- East wall (exterior faces east, x=const):  wall x_coord = slab_x - 0.3125

```python
EXT_HALF = 0.3125  # half of 7.5" wall = 3.75"

# Slab boundary at y=8 (south face), wall centerline inset:
make_wall(x0, y=8 + EXT_HALF, x1, y=8 + EXT_HALF, ...)  # south wall

# Slab boundary at y=50 (north face), wall centerline inset:
make_wall(x0, y=50 - EXT_HALF, x1, y=50 - EXT_HALF, ...)  # north wall

# Slab boundary at x=0 (west face):
make_wall(x=0 + EXT_HALF, y0, x=0 + EXT_HALF, y1, ...)  # west wall

# Slab boundary at x=90 (east face):
make_wall(x=90 - EXT_HALF, y0, x=90 - EXT_HALF, y1, ...)  # east wall
```

### Do NOT use location_line="exterior"
Remove it from all wall creation calls — it does nothing useful and adds confusion.

## Section 32 — Roof Overhang Standards (CONFIRMED 2026-03-08)

## Section 37 — No Overlapping Doors/Windows on Exterior Walls (CONFIRMED 2026-03-08)

### Rule
Before placing any window, verify it does not overlap any door on the same wall.
Before placing any door, verify it does not overlap any window already on the same wall.

### Clearance Formula
```python
# Minimum clear gap between any two openings = 1ft
# Door half-widths: 36" door = 1.5ft, 8ft slider = 4ft, 6ft slider = 3ft, 16ft OH = 8ft
# Window half-widths: 72" = 3ft, 60" = 2.5ft, 48" = 2ft, 24" = 1ft

def openings_clear(pos_a, half_a, pos_b, half_b, gap=1.0):
    return abs(pos_a - pos_b) >= (half_a + half_b + gap)

# Example: 8ft slider at y=40, 48" window at y=35 on same wall:
# abs(40-35) = 5 >= (4 + 2 + 1) = 7 → FALSE, they overlap ❌
# Move window to y=30: abs(40-30) = 10 >= 7 → TRUE ✅
```

|---------|-----------|

| 6ft slider | 3.0ft |
| 8ft slider | 4.0ft |
| 144" (12ft) multi-panel | 6.0ft |
| 16ft OH garage door | 8.0ft |
| 72" window | 3.0ft |
| 60" window | 2.5ft |
| 48" window | 2.0ft |
| 36" window | 1.5ft |

### Mandatory Check in Stage 2 Script

wall_openings = {}  # {wall_id: [(center_pos, half_width, label)]}

## Section 40 — Clerestory Window Z is Absolute from Level 1.0 (CONFIRMED 2026-03-08)

Window `z` (sill height) is always measured from Level 1.0 (z=0), regardless of what level the host wall was created on.

```python

#   z = 9.0 + 0.5 = 9.5  (0.5ft above clerestory base)

#   z = 9.0 + 3.0 = 12.0 (3ft above base, top at 14ft)

# e.g. wing_h=9, offset=3 → z=12
```
